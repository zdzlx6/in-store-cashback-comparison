## The Quiet Rise of In-Store Cash Back

For a long time, the assumption was that you couldn't really do cash back at a physical store without involving the merchant directly. Loyalty programs like Target Circle, Kohl's Cash, and CVS ExtraCare handle that, but they're locked to a single retailer. What people wanted was something more like the online cash back model — one app, many stores, no commitment to any of them.

The breakthrough was card-linking. Instead of trying to integrate with each store's point-of-sale system, a few platforms decided to work one level up: they let you register your existing credit or debit card with the app, then tracked purchases automatically when you used that card at participating merchants. No coupon to show the cashier, no barcode to scan, no extra step at checkout. You just pay like normal and the cash back tracks in the background.

That model has matured into a real category. The five apps that consistently come up in this conversation — TopCashback, Rakuten, Ibotta, Fetch, and RetailMeNot — take notably different approaches to the same problem. Some are card-linking first, some are receipt-scan first, some are online-first with an in-store extension. Understanding the difference is the whole game.

## How the In-Store Models Actually Differ

Before getting into the apps themselves, it's worth being clear about the three mechanical models you'll encounter. They sound similar but they place very different demands on you as a shopper.

**Card-linked offers** are the lowest-friction option. You add your card once, activate any offers you want, and then just pay with that card. The app handles the rest. The trade-off is that offers tend to be specific — "5% back at Walgreens through Sunday" rather than an across-the-board percentage — and the selection of participating merchants changes regularly.

**Receipt-scan offers** flip the workload. You don't have to pre-link anything, but after each shopping trip you have to photograph your receipt and submit it through the app. The upside is broader compatibility — almost any grocery or drugstore receipt will work — but the downside is that it requires discipline. Miss a receipt and you've missed the cash back.

**Online-portal in-store extensions** sit somewhere in between. The app is fundamentally an online cash back portal, but it has an in-store component (usually card-linked) that extends the same account to physical purchases. This is where TopCashback lives, and it's a meaningful differentiator — the same balance, the same payout options, the same single account covers both online and in-store activity.
